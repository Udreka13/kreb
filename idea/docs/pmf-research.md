# kreb — PMF research findings

**Date:** 2026-08-13/14
**Method:** 8 parallel `hermes` agents (deepseek-v4-flash, DDG + HN Algolia + live `curl`), one per question. Decision-critical claims independently re-verified by me. Raw reports in scratchpad (`q1`–`q8`), listed at the end.

**Verdict in one line:** the core artifact is real, unoccupied, cheap, and buildable — but the business shape around it is refuted by history, and two of the three renderers are unsupported by evidence.

---

## 1. Is it worth building in this shape?

**The core: yes.** The thesis is not just unoccupied, it is *validated by the incumbents' documented failure mode.*

The free auto-docs wave (DeepWiki et al.) is being publicly savaged for exactly the four things kreb's §6.2/§6.3 are engineered to prevent:

| Complaint against DeepWiki | kreb's answer |
|---|---|
| "It frequently produces hallucinations and factual errors… presented confidently as authoritative documentation" — OpenWrt maintainer, 2026-04-26 | confidence tiers, `verified` read off the AST |
| "once it has generated its Wiki page… it does not get regenerated if the repo is updated… will quite rapidly become stale" — same | symbol-anchored staleness (§6.3) |
| LibreOffice page falsely claimed Buck as the primary build system; LLVM pages "ranged from incomplete to just plain incorrect" | Gate A: 0 fabricated anchors, hard fail |
| "I received an AI-slop bug report… generated by DeepWiki. It was very incorrect… wasted about an hour" — PureLB maintainer | evidence chain per claim |

That is a rare position: the dominant free competitor's most-cited weakness is precisely your differentiator. The backlash is your marketing copy.

**The shape: no, three things are wrong.**

### 1a. The monetization model is refuted by an unbroken run of corpses

This is the strongest finding in the whole sweep, and it is not close.

- **Kite** — 500k MAU, shut down 2021. Founder Adam Smith: *"Our diagnosis is that individual developers do not pay for tools. Their manager might, but engineering managers only want to pay for discrete new capabilities, i.e. making their developers 18% faster when writing code did not resonate strongly enough."*
- **Bloop.ai → Vibe Kanban** — shut down **2026-04-10**, four months ago. Founder: *"Thousands of software engineers use Vibe Kanban every day… but the vast majority are free users and we couldn't find a business model that we could get excited about."*
- **CodeSee** — $10M raised, $10M burned, "struggled to convert free users to paid," acqui-hired into GitKraken 2024. Post-mortem framing: *"building a vitamin when the market demanded painkillers."*
- **Onboard AI** — getonboard.dev returns HTTP 000. Dead, no post-mortem.
- **Watermelon** — pivoted out of code-docs into enterprise reliability.
- **Sourcegraph** — killed Cody Free *and* Cody Pro on 2025-07-23. Only Enterprise remains, from $16K/yr.

Every published per-developer price in this category is **$19–$40/dev/mo** (Unblocked $19/$29, Greptile Pro $30, Qodo ~$30, Swimm $39) — and every one of those is sold to *teams*. The individual tier is free at Komment, Greptile, Mintlify, and Swimm. Not one profitable individually-priced code-comprehension product was found; the agent searched for a counterexample and reported the absence explicitly.

The pattern is sharper than "individuals don't pay." Individuals demonstrably *do* pay out of pocket — Copilot $10, Cursor $20, JetBrains, Claude/ChatGPT $20. The dividing line is **discrete high-frequency capability** (write code faster, used every day) versus **abstract informational value** (understand code, used at onboarding and in spiky moments). kreb is squarely on the losing side of that line, and Swimm raised $27.6M in exactly this niche without proving it.

### 1b. The problem framing over-weights the wrong pain

Of §1's three costs, the evidence is lopsided:

- **Cognitive overload — very strong and escalating.** ~12 distinct 2025–26 HN threads. *"Sure, I could churn out code 10x as fast, but I still have to review it. I still have to think."* (451 pts, 2026-01-08). Ladybird's *"tsunami of slop overwhelms core maintainers"* (900 pts, 2026-06-05). Willison, 2025-01: *"the alleged author does not actually understand what the code does."*
- **The "why" question — strong but older and more generic.** Best match: the maintainable.fm episode submitted to HN 2026-04-01 titled *"'Why does this code look like this?' Nobody knows. That's the problem."* Chesterton's Fence recurs at 150–200 pts every couple of years.
- **Eye strain — essentially absent as an AI-era problem.** The threads that exist are medical/ergonomic and pre-date AI; nobody frames eye strain as caused by reading unfamiliar AI-generated code. §1 should drop it rather than defend it.

**Counter-signal worth taking seriously:** *"people do not want documentation, they want to ask someone that knows"* (HN, 2025-04-05), and *"outdated Docs (Coding Agents often don't even look at them)"* (2026-07-07). The thing developers hate is *static auto-doc dumps that go stale* — which is the failure kreb's staleness tracking exists to prevent. The counter-signal argues for keeping staleness, not for abandoning the product.

### 1c. The MCP surface plan does not survive contact with the clients

The PRD's protocol research is **accurate** — I verified this directly, not just via the agents. SEP-2577 is real (Final, created 2026-04-14), it deprecates Roots, Sampling *and* Logging with a ≥12-month window, and it explicitly directs servers to integrate with provider APIs instead. The 2026-07-28 revision is real and current. Tasks did move out of experimental. Elicitation is not deprecated.

But the *client* reality undercuts §4:

- **opencode — the PRD's "first native adapter" and daily driver — supports none of it.** No sampling (capability commented out, issue #11948), no elicitation (#23066), no tasks (#28567). Only roots.
- **No MCP client implements the tasks extension at all** per the extensions client matrix. §4.3's async design has no host to run on today.
- **MCP prompts as slash commands work in Claude Code but NOT in VS Code or Cursor** (official matrix: Prompts ❌ for both). §4.1's "primary surface" reaches one host, not four.
- The 2026-07-28 revision is a deeper rewrite than the PRD registers: the protocol went **stateless** — no `initialize` handshake, no `Mcp-Session-Id`, per-request `_meta`, and server-initiated elicitation folded into the MRTR pattern.

This doesn't kill anything — it vindicates §3.3's decision that **the CLI is the real integration boundary.** It does mean the MCP server should drop from "primary surface" to "one adapter among several, shipped when clients catch up."

## 2. What is redundant?

**Cut outright:**

- **The video renderer.** The sharpest analytical finding in the sweep: video is *not* a dumb transformation. The doc contains no slide breaks, no diagram placement, no on-screen pacing — so video is a second authoring surface with its own content model, which breaks the PRD's own "all value lives in the doc" premise at exactly one place. It is also the largest scope item. Demand evidence is near-zero: the only repo→video Show HN found scored 3 points, and even the commercial vendor in the space (VidNo) concedes "not every PR warrants a video." The DITA community abandoned the one-source-fits-all-renderings ideal for this reason; Sphinx only sustains it because all its outputs are linear reflows of the same prose.
- **The `domain` web-research mode.** Unbounded, speculative, and the injection surface §12 already worries about. `deps` (lockfile-allowlisted) carries most of the value.
- **The second research driver (`host`).** Two parallel research paths means duplicated prompt and cost logic for one developer, and §6.1's own table shows `host` loses async, CI, and context hygiene. Pick `engine`.
- **The native opencode plugin.** CLI + MCP + plugin is three surfaces for a solo dev; the plugin is the least-used and highest-maintenance.
- **Eye strain from §1.** Not evidenced; weakens an otherwise strong problem statement.

**Demote:**

- **Audio.** Weak but cheap. Every repo→podcast tool is a hobby project — GitPodcast has 813 stars against gitingest's 15,298, roughly 20× smaller in the same demand space. Developers do listen to technical audio, but to *expert interviews*, not TTS narration of a repo. And there's a real learning-science objection: passive audio is a weak channel for first-time comprehension of dense material — which §8's own scoping already half-concedes. Keep it only if it stays a literal TTS call over the doc; defer otherwise.
- **Dual-language symbol anchoring (Python + TS/JS).** Two grammar ecosystems at once. Ship one, add the second when the first is proven.
- **Budget accounting and preflight estimates.** Right instinct, but a hard cap is enough for v1; the full accounting subsystem can wait.

**Never cut:** symbol anchoring + confidence tiers + git-history archaeology. That triad is the *entire* defence against "just ask your coding agent," and it is the thing every competitor lacks.

**The redundancy risk that matters most** — and the agent was honest that it could not resolve it: a developer with Claude Code already has a terminal and can run `git log -S`, `git blame`, `gh pr view`. No HN thread was found either praising or faulting agents at git archaeology, which is a suspicious silence in both directions. If the real workflow is "ask the agent one question at a time," kreb's wedge is not *doing* the archaeology but *automating it repo-wide into one bounded, citable artifact.* **This is the single most important unverified assumption in the research and the first thing to test by hand.**

## 3. Is there direct competition?

**No one ships the exact artifact.** Searched across ~30 tools; nothing combines symbol-anchored citations + confidence tiers + why-from-git + staleness in a persistent document.

The landscape sorts into three groups:

- **The free wave — the real threat.** DeepWiki (free, ~50k repos pre-indexed, free MCP server, private repos via paid Devin), GitDiagram (15.9k★), gitingest (15.3k★), repomix (27.8k★), Context7 (60.7k★), Grok-Wiki (local-first DeepWiki descendant). You cannot win on "free auto-doc generation" — only on trust. **If Cognition adds confidence labels and staleness to DeepWiki, the niche compresses to zero.** That is the main strategic risk to watch.
- **The funded adjacents — all pointed elsewhere.** Unblocked ($20M Series A, 2025-05) is ~90% aligned on the "why" thesis but sells org-context to teams. Greptile ($30/seat) went to PR review. Sourcegraph went enterprise-only. Swimm ($27.6M) is struggling. Notably, **none publishes a standalone explainer artifact** — that's the open lane.
- **Prior art on the core, with zero traction.** `gitwhy` (haruaki-nayuta/gitwhy, Rust) implements blame-through + pickaxe verification + PR/issue evidence chains with high/speculative confidence labels — nearly the PRD's thesis verbatim. **I checked it directly: 0 stars, last pushed 2026-06-08.** Read that correctly: not a competitor, but proof the hard part is buildable, and a reuse candidate.

## 4. Is it possible to implement?

**Yes, on both axes, and this is the least worrying question.**

**Cost — under $2 is realistic, with one condition.** At 2026-08-14 OpenRouter prices, assuming ~1.5M input / 200k output tokens for a depth-2 doc:

| Model | Cost/doc |
|---|---|
| deepseek-v4-flash ($0.08/$0.18), no cache | **~$0.16** |
| deepseek-v4-pro ($0.435/$0.87), 60% cache reads | **~$0.44** |
| claude-sonnet-4.6 ($3/$15), with caching | **~$5.07** ← the risk case |

The condition: **the `research` role must be anchored to cheap frontier (DeepSeek, Gemini Flash), not Sonnet/Opus.** §3.5's "frontier model for research" rule of thumb, taken literally, blows the Gate D target by 2.5×. Caching is real (DeepSeek reads ~99% off, Anthropic 90%) but only the stable prefix caches — the growing research log re-bills, exactly as §6.1 point 4 anticipates.

**Buildability — ~4–7 person-months to milestone 1** (symbol index + research loop + typed doc + HTML), ~6–10 for all renderers; realistically 9–14 calendar months alongside a job. It's assembly, not invention, because the two hardest pieces already exist as OSS:

- Symbol index / repo map: Aider's `repomap.py`, Graft, CodeRLM, ast-grep (15.5k★, active)
- Git archaeology: `gitwhy`
- ⚠️ **`github/stack-graphs` is ARCHIVED** (2025-09-09, "no longer supported or updated by GitHub"). The most-cited ready-made answer for cross-file name resolution is a dead dependency — mine it for ideas, don't import it. `tree-sitter-graph` is stale too (last push 2024-12).

Also worth noting: §6.1's "navigate, don't embed" call is independently corroborated. Cline wrote up why they deliberately don't vector-index, and the Claude Code team dropped embeddings-RAG in favour of filesystem exploration.

## Recommendation

Build it, but change the shape:

1. **Ship the CLI first and alone.** §14 already sequences this correctly; the MCP client reality makes it doubly right.
2. **Cut video. Defer audio. Cut `host` driver, `domain` mode, the opencode plugin, and one of the two languages.** This turns a 9–14 month project into something closer to 4–6.
3. **Stop planning to charge individuals $2/doc.** Bring-your-own-key and free is right for adoption; if this ever monetizes it will be to engineering managers as "make my team faster on AI-generated code" — the discrete-capability pitch Kite said managers *do* buy.
4. **Test the redundancy assumption by hand this week, before writing code.** Take a repo you know, ask Claude Code a hard "why" question with terminal access, and compare against what kreb would produce. If the agent wins, the wedge is repo-wide automation, not archaeology — and that changes the design.
5. **Gate B is the right gate and it is the *only* one that matters.** Keep it exactly as §10.2 specifies.

The honest summary: **you have found a real gap that the market's dominant free player is actively creating for you, and you can build it cheaply — but you are building it for users who have never once paid for this category.** That's a fine reason to build it anyway; it's a bad reason to plan a business around it.
